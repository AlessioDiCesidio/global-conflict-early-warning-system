Model Validation

Performance is assessed using ROC-AUC and Precision-Recall AUC. ROC-AUC measures the model's ability to discriminate between country-month observations that subsequently experience a major escalation and those that do not. PR-AUC is particularly relevant because major escalation events are relatively rare in the dataset. The logistic regression model provides a simpler benchmark against which the Gradient Boosting model is compared.

Operational Thresholds

Conservative vs Early-Warning Mode

The model produces a continuous probability score. Operational decisions require converting this score into an alert using a threshold. The conservative mode uses a threshold of 0.50. It generates fewer alerts and therefore limits false positives, but it detects only a small proportion of observed escalations. The early-warning mode uses a threshold of 0.20. This lower threshold deliberately prioritizes recall: more potential escalations are flagged, at the cost of generating more false alarms. The appropriate threshold therefore depends on the operational cost of missed escalations versus false warnings.

Countries Ranked by Predicted Risk

Countries are ranked according to the model's predicted six-month escalation probability using their latest available observation. A higher score indicates that the observed characteristics of the country-month resemble patterns that the model associates with subsequent major escalation.

The ranking should be interpreted as a prioritization tool rather than as a deterministic forecast. A high predicted probability does not imply that an escalation will necessarily occur.

Global Conflict Risk

This map shows the model's predicted probability of major conflict escalationù within the next six months using the latest available country-level observation in the dataset. It represents a forward-looking model signal rather than a measure of current conflict intensity.

6-Month Early-Warning Risk

The map shows the model's predicted probability of a major conflict escalation within the next six months. The 0.20 threshold is used as the operational early-warning threshold. Country-month observations at or above this threshold are treated as potential early-warning alerts. The map should not be interpreted as a map of current conflict severity. A country can have high current conflict intensity but low predicted escalation risk, or relatively low current intensity but elevated predicted risk.

Five-Year Conflict Risk Projection

This interactive map presents a five-year baseline scenario for 2026-2030. The model's latest available country-level characteristics are held constant throughout the projection horizon. The resulting values therefore represent a scenario analysis of how the model's estimated escalation risk would accumulate over repeated six-month periods if current conditions remained unchanged. The projection is not a direct forecast of geopolitical events and should not be interpreted as a prediction that today's risk conditions will actually persist for five years. The cumulative risk represents the estimated probability of at least one escalation occurring over the corresponding projection horizon under this constant-conditions scenario.

Observed Major Escalation

This map represents observed conflict intensity rather than model-predicted escalation risk. Fatalities per 100,000 inhabitants are used to provide a population-adjusted measure of conflict intensity. This measure answers a different question from the early-warning map: it describes the intensity of conflict already observed in the data rather than the probability of a future escalation.

Methodological Notes

Unit of analysis

The model operates at the country-month level. Each observation represents one country in one calendar month. The dataset combines conflict-related, socioeconomic and demographic indicators to characterize the conditions observed at that point in time.

Prediction target

The prediction target identifies whether a major conflict escalation occurs within the subsequent six-month period. The model therefore uses information available at a country-month observation to estimate the probability of a subsequent escalation.

Temporal validation

The model is evaluated using a chronological train-test split rather than a random split. The training period covers 1989-2019, while the out-of-sample test period covers 2020-2025.

This temporal design prevents observations from the future test period from being used to train the model and provides a more realistic assessment of prospective predictive performance.

Models

The primary model is a Gradient Boosting Classifier. A Logistic Regression model is used as a benchmark to determine whether the nonlinear Gradient Boosting approach provides additional predictive value.

Evaluation metrics

Model discrimination is evaluated using ROC-AUC and PR-AUC. ROC-AUC measures how effectively the model ranks observations that subsequently experience escalation above those that do not. PR-AUC is particularly informative in this application because major escalation events represent a minority of country-month observations.

Operational thresholds

The model produces a continuous probability score. Two operational thresholds are presented.

The Conservative mode uses a threshold of 0.50, producing fewer alerts and prioritizing precision. The Early-Warning mode uses a threshold of 0.20, prioritizing the detection of a larger proportion of potential escalations at the cost of more false alarms.

Observed conflict intensity

Observed conflict intensity is measured using fatalities per 100,000 inhabitants. This measure is displayed separately from predicted escalation risk because current conflict intensity and future escalation risk represent different concepts.

Five-year scenario projection

The five-year projection extends the model across ten consecutive six-month periods covering 2026–2030. Because future explanatory variables are unknown, the latest available country-level conditions are held constant throughout the baseline scenario.

Cumulative risk represents the estimated probability of experiencing at least one escalation over the corresponding sequence of six-month periods, assuming that the six-month risk remains constant and the periods are treated as conditionally independent.

The five-year projection is therefore a scenario-based extrapolation, not a literal forecast of future geopolitical, political, economic or military conditions.

Limitations

The model is based on historical relationships between observed country-month characteristics and subsequent conflict escalations. These relationships may not remain stable over time.

Future conflicts may involve political, military, economic, technological or social conditions that are not adequately represented in the historical training data.

The system should therefore be considered an early-warning and analytical decision-support tool, rather than an autonomous conflict prediction system. Predicted probabilities should be interpreted as model-based risk estimates and ranking signals, not deterministic predictions.

How to Read the Results

Model performance

The Gradient Boosting model achieves a ROC-AUC of 0.882, compared with 0.844 for the Logistic Regression benchmark. This indicates stronger out-of-sample discrimination between country-month observations that subsequently experience a major conflict escalation and those that do not.

The Gradient Boosting model also achieves a PR-AUC of 0.287. Because escalation events are relatively rare, PR-AUC provides an important complementary measure of model performance.

Risk ranking and early warning

The model assigns each country-month observation a predicted probability of major conflict escalation within the subsequent six months. These probabilities are primarily useful as a risk-ranking signal: higher values indicate that the observed characteristics more closely resemble historical patterns associated with subsequent escalation.

The early-warning analysis evaluates how effectively observed escalation events are concentrated within the highest-risk observations. Expanding the alert population generally increases recall while reducing precision.

Operational thresholds

The Conservative mode uses a 0.50 threshold and generates fewer alerts. The Early-Warning mode uses a 0.20 threshold and generates more alerts in order to identify a larger proportion of potential escalations.

The two modes therefore represent different operational trade-offs between false alarms and missed escalations. The lower threshold is appropriate when missing a potential escalation is considered more costly than investigating additional alerts.

Global Conflict Risk

The Global Conflict Risk map represents the model's predicted probability of a subsequent major escalation using the latest available country-level observation.

This is a forward-looking model signal, not a direct measure of the amount of violence currently occurring in a country.

Observed Conflict Intensity

The Observed Major Escalation map represents observed conflict intensity using fatalities per 100,000 inhabitants.

It therefore describes the level of conflict-related violence recorded in the data rather than the model's assessment of future escalation risk.

The observed and predicted maps should not be expected to look the same. They answer different questions: observed intensity describes what has been recorded, while predicted risk describes what the model considers more likely to escalate.

Six-month early-warning risk

The 6-Month Early-Warning Risk map shows the model's estimated probability of a major escalation occurring within the following six months.

The operational threshold is set at 0.20. Country-month observations at or above this threshold are treated as potential early-warning alerts.

The threshold is an operational choice rather than a universally optimal or universally calibrated probability boundary. Accordingly, the map should be interpreted as an alerting tool rather than as a definitive classification of countries as "safe" or "unsafe".

Five-year conflict risk projection

The Five-Year Conflict Risk Projection presents a baseline scenario covering 2026–2030.

Because future explanatory variables are unknown, the projection holds the latest available country-level conditions constant. It therefore answers a hypothetical question: what would the model's cumulative escalation risk look like if the current observed conditions were maintained?

The cumulative probability represents the estimated probability of experiencing at least one escalation across the sequence of six-month periods.

Cumulative risk naturally increases as the projection horizon becomes longer. Therefore, a country becoming darker over the five-year horizon does not necessarily mean that its underlying six-month risk is increasing. It can simply reflect the accumulation of risk over multiple periods.

The five-year map should consequently be interpreted as a scenario analysis, not as a literal prediction of geopolitical events between 2026 and 2030.

How the maps should be interpreted together

The four geographical views provide complementary information. The Global Conflict Risk map shows the model's predicted near-term escalation risk. The Observed Conflict Intensity map shows recorded conflict intensity. The 6-Month Early-Warning Risk map converts predicted risk into an operational alert using the 0.20 threshold. The Five-Year Projection illustrates how estimated risk could accumulate over a longer horizon under a constant-conditions scenario.

Differences between these maps are therefore expected and do not necessarily indicate inconsistencies. A country experiencing substantial current violence may receive relatively low additional escalation risk, while a country with limited currently observed violence may receive elevated predicted risk if its characteristics resemble historical pre-escalation patterns.

Final interpretation

The system is designed to help identify countries and periods that warrant closer analytical attention. It is not designed to determine whether a conflict will occur with certainty.

Model outputs should therefore be interpreted alongside qualitative geopolitical analysis, expert assessment and other relevant information. The value of the system lies primarily in systematic risk ranking, early identification of potential escalation and structured monitoring.
